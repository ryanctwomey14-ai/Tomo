Sources of return

Return drivers of the asset class.

Our business plans focus on the return drivers we can influence through operations and capital planning, and we underwrite conservatively on the ones we can't.

  • Current income

    Rental income, net of operating expenses and debt service, is distributed quarterly once a property is stabilized.

  • Operational value creation

    Interior renovations, expense management and professional leasing increase net operating income, which drives valuation.

  • Tax efficiency

    Depreciation, accelerated through cost segregation studies, is allocated to investors on Schedule K‑1 and may offset taxable income from the investment.

  • Inflation linkage

    Leases typically reprice every 12 months, so rental income can adjust to changes in market conditions faster than long-lease property types.

Who is eligible to invest?

Offerings are available to accredited investors as defined in Rule 501 of Regulation D. That generally means individuals with income above $200,000 ($300,000 jointly) in each of the past two years, individuals with net worth above $1 million excluding their primary residence, or certain licensed professionals and entities.

How are offerings structured?

Each property is held in a single-purpose entity. Investors purchase limited partnership or LLC interests through a private placement under Regulation D, Rule 506(c). The minimum investment is typically $50,000 and is set out in each offering.

What fees does the sponsor charge?

Typical terms include an acquisition fee of [X]% of the purchase price, an annual asset management fee of [X]% of collected revenue, and a share of profits above the preferred return. All fees are disclosed in full in each private placement memorandum.

When are distributions paid?

Once a property is stabilized, distributions are typically paid quarterly from operating cash flow. Proceeds from a refinance or sale are distributed according to the waterfall in the operating agreement. Distributions are not guaranteed.

How do you manage downside risk?

We use fixed-rate or rate-capped debt, limit leverage at acquisition, fund reserves at closing, and stress-test each acquisition against higher exit capitalization rates and slower rent growth. When a property underperforms its business plan, investors are informed promptly along with the corrective actions being taken.

All investments involve risk, including loss of principal. Risks are described in full in the offering documents.

What is the tax treatment?

Investors receive a Schedule K‑1 each year. Depreciation, including accelerated depreciation from cost segregation studies, is allocated to investors and may offset some or all of the taxable income from the investment. Tax outcomes depend on individual circumstances. Consult your tax advisor.

Can I invest through a retirement account or entity?

Yes. Investors may subscribe through self-directed IRAs, Solo 401(k)s, trusts, LLCs and family office entities, subject to custodian requirements. Retirement accounts may be subject to UBTI when the property uses leverage.
